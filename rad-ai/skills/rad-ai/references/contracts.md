# Outcome contract

Record outcome, kind (delivered or experiment), acceptance/failures, exclusions, invariants, risk, base/workspace/write scope, consumers, dependencies, checks, time budget, round limit (two unless risk sets another) and authorization. Preserve configured defaults. An experiment's contract states its one question and the closed list of observations that answer it. A general requirement of the owner (clean, fast, explained) applies to delivered outcomes and to the exit of a stage, not to each experiment's record.

Enumerated scope. When acceptance covers a set (components, methods, channels, techniques, states), the contract names the set and the submission carries a coverage table, element by means of coverage, so a gap is visible by reading it. Closure of the table is assessed on every submission; a gap found later is one blocking defect of the table, not a new surprise per round.

Required evidence. The contract names the invariants and acceptance criteria that need discriminating evidence (a check observed to fail when the property is broken) and the observations that must be made on real dependencies. A variation of the code that survives the checks is a finding only when it breaks a named invariant, an acceptance criterion or a critical consequence: blocking for a critical consequence, otherwise a correction whose check is that the variation fails; any other survivor is a note. Name these before implementation; a reviewer who finds the list insufficient reports a contract gap once, with the invariants to add, instead of extending it round by round; the coordinator amends the contract once.

Postponed observations. An observation the candidate cannot make (an unavailable environment, a dependency built later) is postponed only by the contract: the record states what is unobserved, why, the outcome that will observe it and what bounds the risk meanwhile. Reviewers and implementers propose; they never mark it themselves. Postponed observations of a critical consequence need an owner decision.

Documents. Text a delivered outcome adds or changes is in scope: its statements are claims checked against the candidate. A false statement blocks only when it misstates a requirement, the answer or a constraint others rely on; any other inaccuracy is a correction. A submission record is evidence, not a deliverable: check the statements the verdict depends on. Documents record decisions, requirements and limits; text that restates what the code does is removed or replaced by a pointer to the code, because it must be re-verified on every change.

Heuristic checks. A check that cannot be complete (scanner, filter, detection rule) states each residual risk and what bounds it. A residual is a recorded limit only when it is not a reproducible violation of the acceptance criteria and its bound is observable (a test, limit or detection that fails when exceeded); otherwise it is a blocking defect. A stated residual never covers a critical consequence.

Existing records hold outcome ID, requirements/revision, state, review history with rounds and observed cost, evidence including the reviewer's own observations, open corrections with their checks, postponed observations, decisions and next step. Every outcome accepted separately has its own record; a record coarser than its outcomes misstates progress. Completion means acceptance with every correction closed on the final revision, including required delivery; reopening reduces completion. Architectural decisions record alternatives, consequences and reconsideration conditions.

## Findings

Scope covers the owner request, project rules, affected scenarios and invariants beyond the written contract. A review sorts every finding into one of three kinds.

- **Blocking defect.** In what the outcome delivers: a reproducible violation of an owner requirement, a project rule, a named invariant or acceptance criterion, or a critical consequence; a regression in an affected scenario or consumer; a pre-existing defect the change activates or that blocks the outcome; required evidence for any of these that the reviewer could not obtain either; a contract gap that leaves an owner requirement unverified. Only a blocking defect returns an outcome. Nobody defers one.
- **Correction.** Any other flaw in scope that a check can confirm fixed: a wrong statement, a missing record entry, a local fault no requirement covers. The check is a command or observable procedure that fails before the fix and passes after it. The flaw is fixed, the check passes on the final revision, and the coordinator closes it; a fix that changes anything beyond the correction returns to review as a delta. A correction is never left open or moved to later work.
- **Note.** A suggestion, an unrelated pre-existing issue, an observation the requirements do not cover, or a flaw no check can state. It becomes a task where it belongs or is declined with a reason.

An empty finding list alone does not accept: acceptance needs verified requirements and current required checks. Round history belongs to the outcome: a corrected candidate resubmitted after return advances the round once; repeated assessment and the review of a correction's delta do not. The time budget covers the whole outcome, rework and review included. A changed approach after the limit is a new outcome with its own contract; for a repeated class of defect it is one general rule with a coverage table.

## Status input

Fresh stage snapshots supply task IDs/states and decision IDs, resolution state and human-authority requirement. Count IDs once; contradictory duplicates invalidate reporting. States: ready, active, review, blocked, done, cancelled. Active/review count as in-flight; only current completion is done. Count unresolved human-only decisions once even when they block several tasks. Derive values on every report; source records remain authoritative.
