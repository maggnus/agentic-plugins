# `brief`

The phase before the first line of code, in one file:
[`skills/brief/SKILL.md`](skills/brief/SKILL.md). No references, no templates, no scripts, no state
directory.

It answers four questions and leaves three things behind. The questions: what is being built, what
is deliberately not being built, what would prove it was built, and what is built first. The
outputs: a concept — one claim, the consumer's task, the shortest already-useful path, the refusals,
an observable measure, the unproven assumptions; an order of slices whose first one carries a
consumer end to end; and the documentation frame every later decision is read from — one entry
point, the normative product document, decision records that keep their rejected alternatives, and a
numbered invariant registry, with the authority order between them fixed.

Two rules do most of the work. A capability enters the product only when at least two genuinely
unlike uses require it, or when it is a safety or consistency invariant nothing but the product
itself can enforce — otherwise it is one use's private tool with a general name. And the concept is
finished by capability rather than by length: when the first slice can be named and someone can say
how they would know it works, it is done.

What it never does: fill in a decision that belongs to the owner, track work, or land a change.
Direction, pricing, money, legal exposure, privacy posture, publication and anything irreversible
are written down as open decisions with the work that waits on them, not quietly answered to make
the concept read as complete.

It is orthogonal to how the work is then carried out. Whoever executes — a fleet, a subagent, a
colleague, you alone — takes the first slice from here and supplies the independent reader this
phase requires; `brief` supplies no roles and executes nothing.

Invoke as `$brief:brief` in Codex or `/brief:brief` in Claude.
